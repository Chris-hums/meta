# Content: The Four Quadrants (weighted for cold webinar traffic)

Step 5 of the framework. Every piece of content in the Hammer Them should
map to exactly one of four quadrants. The weighting below is specific to
**cold** webinar traffic — higher skepticism, lower prior trust than a
warm call funnel — so it leans much heavier on objections than a generic
retargeting content mix would.

## Quadrant 1: Questions — 10% of content

Direct questions prospects have about the offer: what does it do, how does
it work, what's included, price range, timeline. For a webinar funnel,
also: what will I learn on the webinar, who is this for, what should I
prepare before the call.

## Quadrant 2: Second-layer questions — 20% of content

The follow-up questions that surface *after* the first question gets
answered. Example: "how does this work?" → "it's a 6-month coaching
program" → the second-layer question is "what happens after the 6
months?" or "what if I can't attend every session?" For a webinar funnel:
what happens after the webinar, what's the sales call like, what do I need
to bring, how long is the call, what if I'm not ready to buy on the call.

This is where most webinar funnels fail to pre-frame — the webinar answers
the surface question but the follow-up goes unaddressed until the sales
call, where it creates friction and drags down close rate.

## Quadrant 3: Expectations — 30% of content

What success looks like, what the process involves, what the prospect
needs to do, the timeline, the experience. For a webinar funnel: what does
a successful client look like, what's onboarding like, time commitment,
what results to expect and by when, what the investment looks like.

## Quadrant 4: Objections — 40% of content (heaviest weight, cold-webinar specific)

The most common objections that come up on sales calls. Cold webinar
traffic is at peak skepticism — these people found the offer through an
ad, watched a presentation from a stranger, and are now being asked onto a
sales call. Objection content has to be the heaviest slice of the mix.

Common objections for cold webinar funnels:
- "It costs too much" / "I can't afford it"
- "I need to think about it" / "I need to talk to my spouse/partner"
- "Is this a scam?" / "Is this legit?" / "I've been burned before"
- "I'm not sure this will work for my specific situation"
- "I need to do more research" / "Let me check the reviews"
- "I don't have time right now"
- "Can you guarantee results?"

Give each objection at least 2–3 pieces addressing it from different
angles — one educational, one testimonial-based, one logical/analytical.

## IF/THEN content logic

- Fewer than 25 pieces per campaign → not enough; generate more before launching (minimum 25, ideally 30–50).
- All content sits in Quadrant 1 only → missing the highest-impact quadrants. Objections and expectations drive show rate and close rate far more than basic questions, especially for cold traffic.
- No objection-handling content at all → **stop, don't launch.** Cold webinar leads without objection pre-framing show up skeptical and close at lower rates. Generate objection content first.
- Long-form content exists (podcasts, full interviews) → include it even if most content is short-form. A prospect who watches 50% of a 90-minute podcast comes through the call significantly hotter than someone who scrolled past 10 short pieces.
- A piece of content doesn't fit any quadrant → cut it. Don't run content that doesn't serve the pre-framing purpose.
- Negative online reputation exists (Reddit threads, bad reviews, Trustpilot complaints) → add Quadrant 4 content that proactively addresses it — don't wait for the prospect to find it themselves. Standard protocol: a due-diligence video on the confirmation page *plus* objection content in the Hammer Them addressing the known issues honestly.

## Message diversity vs. format diversity

50 pieces of content that all say the same thing is really 1 piece of
content in 50 formats. Diversity of *message* is what matters — each piece
needs to address a genuinely different question, objection, or
expectation. Audit for this before loading a campaign: two reels that both
just say "our program will help you grow" are the same content twice.

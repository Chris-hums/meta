# Frequency, Budget, Timing, and the Free Layers

Covers Steps 6–9 of the framework.

## Step 6: Frequency targets

**Short-form content (reels, shorts, under 3 min):**
- Target frequency: 15–20 per person over the campaign window, measured at the campaign level over a rolling 72-hour window.
- Below 10 → increase budget, or narrow the warm audience retention window (14 → 7 days).
- Above 25 → reduce budget, or widen the retention window (14 → 30 days) to spread frequency across more people.

**Long-form content (podcasts, interviews, 10+ min):**
- Target frequency: 10–20 per person.
- Video Views objective on long-form → expect lower frequency but much higher consumption depth per view.
- A single long-form piece fully consumed can pre-frame harder than 10 short-form pieces scrolled past.

**Email component (runs in parallel, not part of the ad spend):**
| Webinar-to-call window | Emails/day |
|---|---|
| 1–3 days | 3–6 |
| 4–7 days | 2–3 |
| 7–14 days | 1–2 (avoid fatigue/unsubscribes) |

- Content should be value-dense and educational, not hype- or testimonial-stuffed.
- Each email addresses one specific question/objection/expectation from the four quadrants.
- Open rate below 40% → check deliverability (domain reputation, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, sender burnout) *before* blaming content. A fresh sending domain may be needed if the current one is burned from over-sending to webinar lists.

**IF/THEN on frequency:**
- Small warm audience (under 1,000) → hits 15–20 frequency easily at $10–20/day; don't overspend, it burns the audience out.
- Large warm audience (10,000+) → needs significantly more budget to hit target frequency across the whole audience; scale proportionally.
- Frequency climbing but show rate not improving after 2 weeks → the problem is content, not frequency. Audit against the four quadrants and check message diversity.
- Show rate improving in week 1 → don't change anything, let it run. Don't subtract content from something that's working.

## Step 7: Budget allocation

**Starting budget by weekly registrant volume:**
| Weekly registrants | Budget |
|---|---|
| Under 100 | $10–20/day total, both windows combined |
| 100–300 | $20–30/day Window 1 + $20–30/day Window 2 ($40–60/day total) |
| 300–500 | $30–50/day each window ($60–100/day total) |
| 500–1,000 | $50–80/day each window ($100–160/day total) |
| 1,000+ | $80–200+/day per window depending on total audience size |

**Three-campaign split (short-form W1, short-form W2, long-form):**
- Default: 35% / 35% / 30%.
- If CRM data shows long-form consumers close at a higher rate → shift toward 30% / 30% / 40%.

**Budget relative to front-end spend:** if cold acquisition ads are
already running (the ads driving webinar registration), Hammer Them budget
should be roughly **10–20% of front-end spend** — e.g. $1,000/day cold
acquisition → $100–200/day Hammer Them; $5,000/day → $500–1,000/day.

**Scaling logic:**
1. Start at the minimum for the registrant volume.
2. Let it run 7 days minimum before touching anything.
3. Show rate or close rate improves → scale budget up 20–30%, run another 7 days.
4. No improvement after 14 days → audit content, not budget. Content is the variable, not spend.
5. Never double budget overnight — frequency spikes past 25 and burns the audience. Scale in 20–30% increments only.
6. Front-end webinar campaign pauses or stops spending → the warm audience stops refreshing and will eventually exhaust. Monitor audience size weekly; below 500 active → reduce budget accordingly.

**Budget edge cases:**
- Running Hammer Them with no front-end cold campaign (retargeting organic traffic — IG followers, YouTube subs, podcast listeners) → valid, but budget should be lower ($10–30/day) since organic audiences are smaller and more engaged.
- Front-end funnel ROI already tight (ROAS under 2x) → start at the floor ($10–20/day), scale only on measurable show-rate improvement. Don't add cost to an already margin-thin funnel.
- Webinar replay available → add a 50%+ replay-watcher video-view audience to the warm audience; they're already pre-framed and need less budget to push over the edge.

## Step 8: Timing and sales-cycle alignment

- The Hammer Them **runs continuously** for a weekly cold webinar funnel — it does not pause between webinars, because there is always someone in Window 1 or Window 2.
- Default warm-audience retention: 14 days.
  - Registrants typically attend within 3 days → 7 days.
  - Calls happen within 3 days of booking → 7 days for Window 2.
  - Sales cycle extends past 14 days (calls booked 2+ weeks out) → expand to 30 days.
- Weekly refresh cycle: new registrants/bookers enter each week; old ones (no-shows) age out after the retention window. No need to pause or restart the campaign — it self-refreshes.

## Step 9: Integration with the free layers

The Hammer Them is the *paid* layer on top of three free ones. All four
run together — the system is incomplete (and show rate suffers) if any one
is missing.

**Confirmation page (free):**
- Registration confirmation page → breakout videos addressing key questions.
- Call booking confirmation page → breakout videos on what to expect on the call.
- Short (1–3 min each), value-dense, address top objections.
- No breakout videos yet → build the confirmation page first, then layer the Hammer Them on top; without it, the ads have to work harder to compensate.

**Value-dense email sequence (free):** runs in parallel with the ads,
3–6/day depending on window length (see Step 6). Redundancy between email
and ad content is fine — reinforcement across channels, not waste.

**Setter/SDR outreach (free):** if setters exist, they should be texting
and calling registrants/bookers between booking and the call.

Setter outreach + Hammer Them ads + email sequence + confirmation page =
the full backend selling system stack.
